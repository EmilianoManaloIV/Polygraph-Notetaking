___
**Experimental Variables**
* **Manipulated Variables:** Researcher assigns participants to levels of the variable
* **Measured Variable:** Researcher records what happens
* **Independent (Manipulated):** Groups, levels, condition
* **Dependent Variable (Measured):** 
* **Control Variable:** Things that are consistent for either measurement
___
**Support For Causal Claims**
* **Covariance IV:** 
	* Treatment Groups (treatment group)
	* Comparison Group (comparison condition)
	* Control Group (no treatment condition)
	* Placebo Group (placebo control group)
* **Temporal Precedence:** Cause variables comes before the effect variables
* **Internal Validity:** Well-designed experiments rule out alternative explanations
	* Design Confound: When a second variable varies systematically along with the IV
		* Systematic Variability: Design confound
		* Unsystematic Variability: Randomly distributed across groups
	* Selection Effect: Participants in one level of the IV are systematically different than participants in the other level of the IV
	* Matched Groups: Uneven groups from the start of the random selection, can use multilevel 
___
**Independent-Groups Designs**
* **Independent-Groups Design:** Different groups of participants are placed at different levels of IV
* **Within-Groups Design:** each participant is presented with all levels of the IV.
* **Posttest-Only Design:** Participants are randomly assigned to IV groups and are tests on the DV just once.
* **Pretest/Posttest Design:** participants DV are measured before experiment and again after the experiment. Gives us an accurate measurement of change. 
___
**Within-Groups Design**
* **Concurrent-Measures Designs:** Participants are exposed to all levels of the IV at the same time, and preference is the DV.
* **Repeated-Measures Design:** Ps are measured on the DV after exposure of each level of the IV.
* Pros: Participants serve as control, increases power to detect differences between conditions, requires fewer participants.
* Cons: Prior condition may effect post-test measurement, fatigue effect may also effect post-test measurements, may not be practical, inter-related variables may effect post-test measurements too.
* **Counterbalancing:** presenting levels of the IV to participants in different orders.
___
**Interrogating Causal Claims**
* Dependent Variables: How well where they measured?
* Independent Variables: How well were they manipulated?
	* Manipulation Check: an extra DV is added to quantify how well a manipulation worked.
	* Pilot Study: Uses a separate groups of Ps to confirm the effectives of the IV manipulation
* External Validity: Is this applicable to the population as a whole?
* Internal Validity: Is there other possible confounds?
