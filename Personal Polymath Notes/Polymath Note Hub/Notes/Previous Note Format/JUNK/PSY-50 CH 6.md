**Choosing Question Formats**
* **Open ended questions**: respondent can give anything, very *broad* response.
* **Forced Choice Format**: provides choices narrows questions, however, *you* have to write all possibilities.
* **Likert Scale:** Strong disagree-strongly agree
* **Semantic Differential Format:** Easiness vs Helpfulness vs Confusing
___
**Consider Wording**
* Be clear and concise, bare essentials and direct.
* Should be basic reading levels (average grade 6), accessible to all populations based on intelligence. 
___
**Writing Well-Worded Questions**
* Needs to be an unbiased question and focuses on one factor that can be measured.
* Order to questions can effect how participants respond.
___
**Encouraging Accurate Responses**
* Lets the responder be efficient but not negligent to their response.
* **RESPONSE SET**
	* **Acquiescence:** Lazy response to a a question (Yea-Saying)
	* **Fence Sitting:** There is a risk when people are undecided, which have no opinion.
___
**Problems With Answers**
* When a response doesn't cover all options or overlaps over other questions.
* **Incongruent Answers:** Improper response to question
* **False Dichotomies:** Can only be the extremes.
* **Double-Barreled Answer:** Two responses for the same question.
___
**Encouraging Accurate Responses: Faking And Self-Reporting**
* **Social Desirability:** People responding in a way to look good in society.
* **Lie Scales:** A test of different personalities that determines personality.
* **Faking Good/Bad:** Faking survey's and questionnaires that are not accurate to what the person actually responds with.
* **Self Reporting Memories:** Some basic calculations that are not accurate to what you can scientifically recollect (Influenced by many factors).
___
**Check With Others And Pilot Test**
* Other surveys that have high validity can be used as a basis of a custom survey.
* Those asking the question can have tunnel vision.
* Its okay to ask others for thoughts.
