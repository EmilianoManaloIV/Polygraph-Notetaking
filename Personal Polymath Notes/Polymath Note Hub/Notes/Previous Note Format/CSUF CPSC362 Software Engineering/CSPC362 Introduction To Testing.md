___
**Objective Of Testing**
* Utilized to maintain the Quality Assurance of the software that is currently in its lifetime.
* QA (how is a product being created) & Testing (specific function of a product) teams are often merged into one, however their behaviors are completely different.
* Ladder of issues:
	* **Error:** Manual issue done by a developer
	* **Bug:** Occurs due to an error
	* **Defect:** Function is giving an unexpected output due to the bug
___
**Who Should Test The Software?**
* Developers tend to ignore bugs. independent testers work towards being a more holistic testing methodology.
___
**Verification & Validation**
* Are we building the product correctly? 
* Is the product following what we want?
___
**V-Model**
* Test the relationship of the system after system engineering, analysis, design, and coding. Then pull the testing cases you need.
___
**Why Do We Have To Test?**
* Because systems may be critical in human life and cause problems or even catastrophic issues.
___
**Benefits**
* Cost effective and may aid in future headaches.
___
**Software Testing Principles**
* Context-Dependent: entirely depends on the requirements of the tested application.
* Absence Of Error Fallacy: An error may be found outside the scope of the developers.
* Early Testing: Helps in finding errors early 
* Pesticide Paradox: Find a different test that aids with edge cases and results.
* Defect Clustering: You should test certain components that can expand to other subsystems.
* Exhaustive Tests Are Impossible: Focus on the primary functionality of an application, not all things can be tested.
___
**Types Of Testing**
* **Manual** Need to do something to see result
* **Automatic** Let a script do repetitive actions
* **Blackbox** Testing the functionality, the code itself
* **Whitebox** Deals with the internal structure of the code
* **Functional Testing:** Tests the functionality of the program
* **Non-Functional Testing:** Tests the non-functional requirements of the system.
___
**Unit Testing**
* Test case per-piece of code, white-box testing
___
**Integration Testing**
* How the modules are integrated with each other?
* Communication between components
	* Top Down Integration: Modules are integrated by the hierarchy.
	* Bottom Up Integration: Testing from the bottom hierarchy to the top.
___
**Acceptance Testing**
* How do the end users interact with the application?
* Alpha Testing: Conducted on the developer's site by a consumer.
* Beta Testing: Conducted at one or more customer sites by end-users.
___
**Exhaustive Testing:** Testing every statement that is often impossible due to scope and complexity.
___
**Coverage Testing**
* Measure of far the code was executed.
	* Statements
	* Branch
	* Condition
	* Path
___
**Non-Functional Testing**
* What is the ease of use regarding the application?
* What is the security of the application?
* Smoke Testing: Critical functionalities are tested.
