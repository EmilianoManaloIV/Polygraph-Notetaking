**Waterfall Model**
* Sequential paradigm in software engineering
* **Why?**
	* The requirements are consistent and stable, the work flow is linear, when things are done piece by piece in smaller SLDC.
	* Requirement -> Analysis -> Design -> Implementation -> Testing -> Operation & Maintenance
* There is no customer feedback.
* When we need to do a change, we need to do the whole process again.
* **Methodology Used In:** Healthcare, Banking, Aerospace
* **Disadvantages**
	* The customer must have patience and has lack of input.
___
**Spiral Model**
* Quadrant based system, each quadrant is a phase. When given feedback we go back to the center of the spiral and start again; early & later iterations.
* **Why?**
	* Gives better opportunities to refine the system
	* More realistic for large scale projects

**Agile Process**
* Rapid and adaptive response to change
* Effective Communication
* Customers to be integrated onto the team

* Accepting any changes to the requirements to any part to the cycle.
	* We make the changes that are needed on the next version after we plan it out.
* There is cross disciplines that communicate to one another, there is collaboration of disciplines for a certain goal.
* **TRADITIONAL MONOLITHIC:**
	* Push bug fixes for all services and operations
* **MICROSERVICES:** 
	* Push bug for only that certain service and operation

**The Agile Manifesto**
1. Customer satisfaction and constant feedback: communication with your consumer.
2. Welcoming changes that are introduced in the development cycle.
3. Working software should be launched at a stable and timely manner.
4. Working software needs be quantified by its requirements.
5. There is a constant pace that keeps things running
6. Communicating with the stakeholders, supporting, and secondary team
7. Face to face conversation is ideal and also should be sharing the same location.

**SCRUM**
* In AGILE, feedback is constantly given and iterated upon per released of the system. SCRUM builds upon this.
* **Backlog:** list of requirements the project needs, a stack of **PBI** that has various priority.
* The product owner determines that priority and then sends it to the technical teams, and then they break up the work and implement it.
* There is a priority across the stack when start of the iteration.
* At the end of iteration there is feedback, validation, and other connections to work on the next iteration (summarized review of the current product).
	* Production Environment vs Development Environment
___
**SCRUM ROLLS**
* Consists of 6 or more members.
	* If BUG: Support/Operations Team -> Testing Team -> Business Team -> Develop Team
* Product Owner: Business Team & Management 
	* Communicated to by SCRUM master if there is requirements that are confusing (possible elongating of timeline)
* Scrum Master: Lead in Development, Testing, & Operations
	* Facilitate team members
	* Deals with technical issues and planning
	* Deals with work politics.
* Other rolls such as QA/UAT, that can help support the SCRUM methodology.
* Release Team: Make sure that the application is deployed properly and fill the requirements (Dev/Support/Testing/Business)
* Product Owners: those behind the idea of the product or solution.
___
**SCRUM Master**
* Self organization of each individual team
* To act as a leader (coach) to understand all divisions of the team.
___
**Development Team**
* Implementation of the requirements
* Include other sections of the organization if they are needed.
___
**Developer Paradox**
* There is a cost factor involved and takes too much time.
* We need a well rounded team to fulfill the requirements.

**SCRUM Development Cycle**
* Product Owner has an idea
* Product backlog - requirements and features
	* Grooming: break down the product into its requirement
		* Creating, refining, and determining time effort.
	* Artifacts: documentation and backlogs.
* Sprint Planning - Scrum master chooses the priority of a PBI from the product backlog
	* How much effort will be needed to implement this feature.
* Sprint Backlog - contains user stories (functionality and requirements)
* Daily Scrum & Sprint Execution
	* Do what is needed to fulfill the requirements and possible deliverable product.
* Spring Review & Retrospective
	* Stakeholders review the product
	* Review on relationship with other teams
* The product backlog has bugs, refactors, and other things that need to be utilized to create the product. The backlog is constantly being manipulated.
	* Feature
	* Change
	* Defect
	* Technical 
	* Knowledge
