Often being the support of the other foster kids while the events where transpiring with ICE.
* How does the series’ framing of foster care illuminate its handling of other social identities, including those of race and sexuality?
	* Though I know little of the character's individually, the series frames fostering as a type of salvation for many individuals. At the end of the episode we find that the younger sister of the major character who is under threat of deportation is a foster under the family' care. The series blends this salvation with several foster's with various backgrounds. For example utilizing many scenes with Jude who is often with their boyfriend.
* And how does the episode frame contemporary debates surrounding immigration and deportation, especially given increased  federal immigration enforcement and protests surrounding that enforcement that have significantly  impacted communities both locally and nationally?
	* Honestly for a show that aired in 2018, its shocking how relevant and real that information is often displayed in modern media. The debates of how ICE often split families and treats those who are arrested like they did some heinous crime is shown throughout the show. First by showing the active capture and arrest of the mother and father. The relocation of the younger sister under protective services. And the older sister having to be held up at a church under threat of also being arrested. The constant involvement of the government is also shown by not just ICE, but also the FBI. We can also not forget when they sent media out detailing the holdout at the church and the heavy amount of backlash that came from it.
