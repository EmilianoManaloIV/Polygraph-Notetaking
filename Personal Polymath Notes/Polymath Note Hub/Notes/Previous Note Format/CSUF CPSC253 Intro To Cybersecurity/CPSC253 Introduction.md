**Protection Of Computer Assets**
* **Devices** (hardware that holds data)
* **Network** (how data is transmitted)
* **Applications** (software tools)
* **Intelligence** (information to predict attacks)
* **Data** (stored, transmitted, or protected)
* Any vulnerability (attack) must be secured against, simple or complex attacks must be covered.
___
**CIA Triad**
* **Confidentiality:** Providing access to certain people and not others
	* Authentication Systems
* **Integrity:** Maintaining what is being sent and what is received (accuracy)
	* The lack of manipulating data and improving accuracy
* **Availability:** When is the service "up" or system is "active"
	* Maintaining the service stays active at all times
* **Authenticity:** Being genuine and not false
* **Accountability:** Being able to find where data is being originated
___
**Computer Security**
* **NIST:** The standards security is built upon
* **CIA** that protects assets (Hardware, Software, Firmware) and information being processed, stored, and communicated.
* In short, what are we doing to protect our hardware, software, firmware, and malicious attacks using the CIA triad.

**Attack Levels**
* Low, Moderate, High
* Dictated by how much data is loss, lower amount of data implies less risk being involved in an attack. Since data can be counted in cost, that is also considered how severe the attack may be.
* There is laws and regulations the can determine how severe attacks can be.

**Challenges**
* Several factors can be so wide in scope its difficult to find all preventative measures.
* In developing a system you must consider a branching approach where the security systems protecting assets can also be attacked.
* Create a solution that is feasible and within budget, prepare a solution that is applied as soon as possible. "Better now than later" thinking.
* Constant monitoring is required to prepare and adapt to attacks.
* Attacks are 24/7 and can be circulated from anywhere in the world

* **System Resource** assets

**Why?**
* Owners want to keep their assets maintained and functional and lower risk to said assets
* Hackers want to change or collect assets which is the risk the owners are trying to prevent by deploying countermeasures.
___
**Computer Security**
* Assets + Confidentiality (privacy), Integrity (accuracy), Availability (consistently) Triad = Computer Security
* Corrupted (loss of integrity): data being manipulated in an unexpected way
* Leaky (loss of confidentiality): data being shared to unverified sources
* Unavailable (loss of availability):  the data cannot be accessed or processed
___
**What's A Threat**?
* **Threat**, its a possibility of harm (protentional), an **attack** is the act of utilizing a threat.
	* **Passive Attacks:** Skimming data without manipulating data or effecting the system
	* **Active:** Taking data and manipulating or disrupting the system in anyway.
* There can be multiple types of attacks at a given moment.
___
**Threat Consequences**
* **Unauthorized Disclosure:** Gains access to data they should not have
	* Interception
	* Exposure
	* Inference
	* Intrusion
* **Deception**: Data being sent to the wrong person by they are authorized
	* Masquerade
	* Falsification
	* Repudiation
* **Disruption:** Interrupts or corrupts the system
* **Usurpation:** 
___
**Unauthorized Disclosure: Interception**
* Two individuals authorized, but a middle man skims data off their connected data line.
___
**Deception: Masquerade**
* An unauthorized entity posing as an authorized entity.
___
* **Passive attack** focuses on skimming data, but not manipulating the system or data. Obtaining data focused. Analyzing traffic of data, simply, observing.
* **Active Attack** focuses on disrupting the system, manipulating the string of data or even adding their own nefarious data.
___
**Attack Surface:** where the attack comes from, the origin of the attack.
* Reachable and exploitable by the attacker. Open port sockets, services within system, interfaces, employee, and code.
* **Network Attack Surface**: Vulnerabilities on the network, network protocol services, which can disrupt or skim data (2FA)
* **Software Attack Surface:** software, viruses, web software.
* **Human Attack Surfaces:** Vulnerabilities created by personal or outsiders, social engineering, human error and trusted insiders, betrayal.
___
**Attack Surface Surface And Layering**
**Layering**
* **Deep:** Multi-layered defense strategy (multi-factor authentication)
* **Shallow:** Minimal layers for defense, more vulnerable
**Attack Surface**
* **Small:** Fewer entry points
* **Large:** more entry points
Combination of these things can create a low security risk, medium security risk, high security risk.
___
**Countermeasure:** Deterrent from attacks
* Detecting an attack
* Preventing an attack
* Recover an attack
* Ultimately the job of these analysis is to reduce the risk to assets.

**Security Requirements**
* **Access Control**: the ability to have requirements for certain pieces of data
	* Roles, read/write permissions
* **Awareness And Training:** human attack surfaces and the personal of the company
* **Audit And Accountability:** is there documented proof of manipulation of data
* **Certification, Accreditation, and Security Assessment:** Information that needs to be taught and understood.
* **Configuration Management:** Guidelines to maintaining the security of a system
* **Contingency Planning:** Define strategies when attack occurs that cause disruptions
* **Identification And Authentication:** Required mechanism to verify the identifies of its users.
___
**Computer Security Strategy**
* **Security Policy:** The formal rules and regulation in implementation of cybersecurity rules
* **Security Implementation:** How do we actually apply these rules
* **Assurance:** Was the design implemented as we envision.
* **Evaluation:** Involves testing and formal analytic or mathematical techniques; was this design actually practical.
